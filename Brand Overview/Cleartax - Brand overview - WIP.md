## **ClearTax Brand Book**

### **Brand Foundations**

## **1\. Purpose**

*Why we exist*

### **To make certainty the default state of finance.**

Finance is the one function in a business that is not allowed to be approximately right. Yet certainty has always been something a team earns late, by hand, and only for a moment. Someone reconciles for three weeks to find out what was true last month. A controller signs a number they have checked but cannot see inside. A CFO learns something is wrong because a notice arrived, and not because the system said so.

That is certainty as an achievement, and it costs a business a version of itself every period.

We exist to make it a starting condition. When a business is certain, it stops managing its books and starts using them. It enters a new market without first working out whether it can survive the paperwork. It decides on this week's numbers rather than last quarter's. It finds better work for its finance team than proving the past.

Speed is the smaller part of this. What we are after is settled, so that everything built on top of it can move.

---

## **2\. Brand Mission**

*What we want to achieve*

### **To build the one system that already knows the rules of every market a business operates in.**

The rules will not get easier. Every year brings another jurisdiction, another mandate, another format, another deadline that shifts. That is the direction the world is travelling in and no company gets to reverse it. What can be decided is where the complexity lands.

Most finance teams today run an assembly of tools, local vendors, spreadsheets and manual steps, held together by the few people who know where the joins are. It works until something changes. A rule, a market, a person leaving.

We are building the thing underneath all of it. One system that knows the rules of the market a business is in, and the next one it enters. When a mandate arrives it becomes configuration rather than a project. The standard holds the same in Bengaluru, Riyadh and Berlin.

---

## **3\. Brand Vision**

*Where those achievements lead*

### **The operating system the world's finance teams run on.**

An operating system is the thing you stop noticing. Nobody chooses one every morning. It is what everything else is built on top of.

That is the position we are building toward. The layer a finance team's work assumes, rather than the application they open.

Products will keep changing shape. Modules, platforms, agents, things nobody has named yet. Underneath all of them sits one system that knows the rules of every market a business operates in, holds the truth of its numbers, and stays current without being asked.

We will know we have arrived when the answer to how do you run finance stops being a list of tools and becomes a sentence. We're on ClearTax.

---

## **4\. Brand Promise**

*What we commit to, every customer, every time*

### **It only gets lighter.**

A promise is worth very little if it describes a state you arrive at once. Finance does not hold still. New mandates, new formats, new markets, new versions of rules that were already handled, new entities to fold into the close.

So the only promise worth making is one that renews. Every release, every model, every playbook we build points at the same outcome. The work that took a quarter of someone's year last year should take less of it this year, and less again the year after.

We do not hand over a system and walk away from it. We keep taking weight off, in every market, for as long as a customer is with us. That is the commitment they can hold us to. Today may still be hard. It will not be harder tomorrow.

## **6\. Target Audience**

### **Primary: the CFO and finance leadership**

CFOs and finance directors at enterprises operating across multiple entities and multiple jurisdictions, typically above ₹500 crore or 60 million dollars in revenue, across manufacturing, retail, FMCG, e-commerce, pharma, financial services and hospitality.

What keeps them awake is not tax. It is risk they cannot see. A hard problem is manageable. An invisible one is the exposure sitting in a subsidiary's ledger that surfaces eighteen months later with penalties attached. Around that sits everything else. Working capital locked up in credits nobody claimed. A close that takes ten days when it should take four. A finance team of expensive people doing clerical work. And a growing suspicion that entering the next market will cost more in overhead than it returns in revenue.

What they want is to be able to say one sentence in a board meeting. We are covered, in every market, and I can prove it.

What we sell them is assurance, along with the working capital and the headcount it releases. The language is risk, control, capital and expansion. Never features.

### **Secondary: heads of tax, indirect tax and compliance**

The functional owners who live inside the problem every day, and usually the people who bring ClearTax through the door in the first place. They care about reconciliation accuracy, notice volume, audit readiness, credit recovery, and never being the person who missed a rule change.

Their fear is professional rather than financial. It is being blamed. Sell them precision, defensibility and a clean audit trail. They become the loudest internal advocates we have, provided the product respects their expertise instead of pretending to replace it.

### **Tertiary: CIO, CTO and ERP owners**

The technical gatekeeper who can end the conversation. They care about integration burden, security posture, data residency, uptime, and whether this becomes one more system to look after.

Their fear is a long and messy implementation. Answer it with one API for every mandate, certified connectors for SAP, Oracle and Microsoft Dynamics, SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001, and data that stays in country. Then get out of the way.

### **Worth naming**

Group financial controllers, shared services and global business services heads, the Big Four advisory partners who shape shortlists, and increasingly the chief risk officer, as tax and regulatory exposure climbs the board agenda.

## **7\. Core Values**

**Make the customer win.** Our success is a consequence of theirs. A customer can be well served and still be losing, with capital sitting in credits nobody claimed and a team doing work a machine should do. We are not finished when they are satisfied. We are finished when they are better off.

**Make velocity a habit.** Speed that only appears when something is on fire is not speed. Regulation does not wait for a roadmap, and when a rule moves our customers have weeks rather than quarters. We decide in hours, ship in weeks, and treat a slow answer as a cost.

**Deliver results.** Effort is not achievement. Finance keeps a binary scoreboard. The number was right or it was not. The system was up or it was not. Our customers live under that scoreboard, so we hold ourselves to one as well. Own the outcome rather than the task, and say early when you are going to miss.

**Play for the front of the jersey.** The name on the front matters more than the name on the back. Nobody gets a customer's numbers right across fifty countries alone, so that result belongs to everyone or to nobody. Pass to whoever is better placed. Take the unglamorous work when the team needs it. Never let a customer feel the seams between our functions.

**Be open and curious.** Rules change in fifty countries at once and every customer's business is stranger than any model of it. Certainty is a liability in that environment. Ask before assuming, invite the challenge rather than defend the position, and treat being wrong as information.

## **9\. Brand Attributes**

The personality describes our character. These five words are the working filter. Every piece of design and every line of copy is checked against them, and anything that cannot be traced back to at least one of them does not go out.

**Assured**

Calm under pressure, never dramatic, always in control. Certainty delivered rather than promised. Security and privacy live here, worn openly rather than defended.

Brings in proof stated as fact, real uptime and volume figures, certifications on show, and layouts that feel settled. Rules out hedging, urgency tactics, fear, and fine print doing the headline's job.

**Intelligent**

AI-native and systems-minded. We solve root problems rather than symptoms. This is the attribute carrying our technology step-change, and it earns a distinct visual treatment of its own.

Brings in the AI story led rather than added, Nova Blue and Flux Lime, showing the mechanism, and what is coming next. Rules out science fiction, glowing brains, robots, and AI used as decoration.

**Globally fluent**

India-forged and world-ready. We look like a company built for fifty countries because we are one. India is where compliance is hardest, which is exactly why we are good at this.

Brings in one visual system everywhere, country coverage as headline content, multi-script typography, and India framed as credential. Rules out market-specific variants, anything that reads as regional, and treating global as a subsection.

**Premium**

High standards, visible in the craft. Confident scale, minimal ornamentation, nothing on a page that is not doing a job. Restraint and precision read as expensive. Clutter reads as cheap however much was spent on it.

Brings in generous space, exact alignment, tabular figures, product-led imagery, and a considered finish on every surface. Rules out scrappy execution, decorative elements, stock imagery, and three shades of blue in one deck.

**Straightforward**

Clear language, no fluff, no buzzwords. A partner who makes complex things easy to act on.

Brings in plain English, the point first, specific numbers in place of adjectives, and instructions a person can follow. Rules out jargon, corporate throat-clearing, generic SaaS-speak, and any line that would still work with a competitor's name on it.

## **10\. Tone of Voice**

We write for people who are handing us something that carries real consequence. That sets the register. Clear, exact, confident, and human enough that a finance team feels they are dealing with people rather than a platform.

Five principles.

**Confident**

We state, we do not pitch. We are unapologetic about our ambition and our scale, because both are real. Never arrogant, and never apologetic either.

> Write: The CFO offices of 3,000 brands run on ClearTax.  
> Don't: We're proud to be one of the leading players in tax technology.

**Direct**

Say what we mean, quickly. The point comes first. If a claim needs three qualifying clauses to survive review, the claim is wrong and we should find a better one.

> Write: Your invoices clear in under a second, in every market you operate in.  
> Don't: Our solution is designed to help enterprises reduce compliance risk exposure.

**Intelligent**

Every line holds substance. Depth without lecturing. Our readers are qualified professionals who usually understand their own position better than we do, so we never explain their job back to them.

> Write: You already know your close calendar. We make sure nothing on it slips.  
> Don't: Compliance can be complicated. Let us walk you through the basics.

**Warm**

Plain English, written for a person. Our subject is heavy enough on its own, so the writing around it should be easy to read. Warm does not mean funny. We are a friendly company handling something serious, and the seriousness sets the ceiling.

> Write: Check the mismatches before you close. We've flagged the ones that matter.  
> Don't: Users are advised to reconcile identified discrepancies prior to submission.

**Outcome-driven**

Lead with what changes for the customer's business, then the technology behind it. Name a risk plainly and show the control in the same breath.

> Write: France in 2026\. Germany in 2027\. Both are already built.  
> Don't: We support e-invoicing across multiple countries.

### **One rule worth spelling out**

Our strongest proof is technical. Ten thousand e-invoices a second. One API for every mandate. Data that stays in country. Being approachable never means taking those out.

Be technical about the proof and plain about the language. Big claim, ordinary words.

### **Words that place us**

Some words make us sound like a tool a person operates. Others place us where we belong.

Move away from filing, returns, portal, upload, software, app, users, easy, hassle-free, saves time, get started in minutes.

Move toward controls, assurance, governance, exposure, period close, working capital, credit recovery, audit trail, continuous, jurisdiction, entity, throughput, platform, system.

File your GST returns becomes close the period with every return already validated. Tax software becomes the system finance runs on. Users becomes finance teams, or controllers, or the CFO's office. Saves time becomes takes clerical work off six people.

### **Where the voice flexes**

Website and campaign work takes more rhythm and shorter lines. Product copy stays as plain as it can be. Sales and CFO material leads with the business outcome and lets the technology follow. Support and incident notes are blunt: what happened, what it affects, what we are doing, when it will be fixed. Thought leadership takes a position and does not sell.
