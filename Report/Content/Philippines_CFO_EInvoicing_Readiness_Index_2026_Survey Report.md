# **Foreword**

**The Deadline Moved Once. Most Companies Still Cannot Legally Start.**

On December 31, 2026, covered taxpayers in the Philippines must issue electronic invoices in structured data format under Revenue Regulations No. 11-2025, as amended by Revenue Regulations No. 26-2025. 

The covered population is broad: 

* Every Small, Medium and Large taxpayer engaged in e-commerce or internet transactions  
* Every taxpayer under the jurisdiction of the Large Taxpayers Service  
* Every taxpayer classified as Large under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act  
* Every taxpayer using a Computerized Accounting System, Computerized Books of Accounts with electronic invoicing, or other invoicing software.

This report combines two bodies of evidence. 

* The first is a survey of 200+ Philippine finance leaders across eight readiness benchmarks, which returned an overall Readiness Index of 30.7 out of 100\.  
* The second is field research conducted across the Manila market during August 2026, spanning public remarks by senior BIR officials at the ECCP E-Invoicing and Tax Compliance Forum, four advisory firms, systems integrators, ERP vendors, and the finance and IT leadership of large enterprise taxpayers.

**The survey told us the market is not ready. The field work told us why, and the reason is not the one most vendors are selling against. *It is not the ERP. It is registration.***

Nobody can issue a compliant electronic invoice before their accounting system is CAS-registered and acknowledged by the Bureau. That gate sits upstream of every technical question in this report, and it is the single most underestimated problem in the Philippine market. 

At Beyond, our CFO event in Manila, only 30 to 40% of attendees said they were CAS-registered. The rest were still in process. And the gate re-closes: Migrating an ERP requires re-applying for CAS registration, a finding confirmed independently three times during the week, and indicated by BIR's Information Systems Project Management Service.

***Two findings stopped us cold.***

**Finding 1: The same wound, in three shapes, in one week.**

**What it means.**   
CAS registration doesn't belong to your company. It belongs to one specific system. Swap that system out and the permit doesn't come with you, you go back to the start of the queue.

**The wound.**   
Three companies. Three sectors. One week. All three can produce an invoice; none can produce one the Bureau would recognise as legally issued. Each is set out in full in The CAS Gate.

* A global information services group has moved accounts payable onto its new cloud platform, but still has to issue every billing invoice out of the old system, because the old system is the one with CAS registration. Nobody could tell us when the new one gets registered.  
* A packaging manufacturer runs an SAP instance that was never CAS-registered at all. It invoices today on a loose-leaf permit.  
* A shared services group generates invoices from its cloud ERP that are, in their own words, *"internal documents only."*

**The impact.**   
Every one of these teams is being asked about e-invoicing readiness while sitting behind a gate they haven't cleared. And the gate re-closes: the ERP migration on your 2027 roadmap means re-applying. Confirmed independently three times in a single week, and indicated by the BIR's Information Systems Project Management Service. Which means CAS registration isn't a box you tick once at the start of the project, it's a dependency that has to be re-planned every time the system changes.

**Finding 2: Most companies are solving the wrong problem first.**

Here's the part the survey could never have surfaced.

**The December obligation is narrower than the market fears. The obligation after it is wider than the market hopes.**

What December 31, 2026 actually requires is one thing: generate a signed, structured invoice and issue it to your buyer. That's it. Continuous electronic sales reporting to the Bureau is a separate obligation, arriving later, and expected to start with a small group of large taxpayers while the BIR's own infrastructure catches up.

So two mistakes are being made at once, in opposite directions:

* Teams scoping for real-time reporting by December are building for a requirement that isn't there yet, and burning the budget they need for the one that is.  
* Teams treating the reporting delay as breathing room have misread it completely. Nothing about the December issuance deadline moved.

**Get the sequence wrong and you don't just waste a quarter. You spend it on the wrong build.**

The market's instinct is to plan for more time, and that instinct has a basis (RR No. 26-2025 expressly reserves to the Commissioner the power to extend the compliance period further). However, an expectation is not a plan. The date in force today is December 31, 2026, penalty provisions attach to the obligation itself rather than to any system milestone, and an audit conducted after the deadline examines the periods that preceded it. Practitioners reported substantial penalty assessments across the market during 2025, the clearest available evidence that assessment capacity is already operating.

The CFOs who move now are not making a bet on the timeline at all. They are removing the registration gate, resolving the data gaps that no vendor can invent their way around, and converting a compliance cost into a deduction under the CREATE MORE Act. Those actions pay off whether December holds or slips.

***This report is for the finance leaders who would rather find their gaps earlier than in January.***

# **The Stakes**

Sections 237 and 237-A of the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 12066, require covered taxpayers to issue invoices as structured data that can be extracted electronically and transmitted to the Bureau. Revenue Regulations No. 11-2025 implements those sections. Revenue Regulations No. 26-2025 sets the date at December 31, 2026\.

**What December actually asks for, and what it does not**

This distinction is the most commercially consequential thing a Philippine CFO can get right, and the survey suggests most have not. The mandate has two legs, and only the first is live in December.

| Leg | Obligation | Timing |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Leg 1  ·  Generation and issuance** | Generate a structured electronic invoice, apply the digital signature, and issue it to the buyer. This is what the December 31, 2026 deadline requires. BIR officials at the ECCP forum framed the minimum test plainly: in-scope taxpayers must have at least one valid electronic invoice generated by the start of the year following the deadline. | December 31, 2026 |
| **Leg 2  ·  Electronic sales reporting** | Continuous transmission of invoice data to the Bureau within the prescribed window, with a unique identifier returned. Revenue Regulations No. 26-2025 states this applies once the Bureau establishes a system capable of storing and processing the data, through a separate Revenue Regulation. | Not published |

*Market intelligence, not regulation. Practitioners with direct BIR visibility indicated that the reporting leg is expected to be mandated first for a limited population of large taxpayers, on the order of a couple of hundred companies, with broader application following as the Bureau's receiving system is established. One advisory firm reported that the sending leg is the mandate and the receiving leg is not yet in scope in the Philippines, unlike Malaysia and the European Union where the receive leg follows. Another indicated BIR may adopt batch rather than real-time reporting, which would materially lower the technical bar. None of this is published. Treat it as directional and confirm against the issuance when it lands.*

**Four operational demands of the Philippine mandate**

| Demand | What it actually requires |
| :---- | :---- |
| **CAS registration first** | The gate that sits upstream of everything. An accounting system must be CAS-registered and acknowledged before it can issue compliant electronic invoices. BIR's ISPMS indicated at the ECCP forum that CAS registration updates must be filed when upgrading an ERP. Field evidence puts CAS readiness at roughly 30 to 40% among attending taxpayers at our Manila event. |
| **Structured data at issuance** | The invoice must be system-generated structured data, not a document that looks digital. RR No. 11-2025 is explicit that a photo or scanned copy of a paper invoice is not an electronic invoice, and that a CAS or POS output printed for the buyer without electronic reporting capability is a traditional manual invoice. BIR officials inidcated JSON as the format, with XML also accepted, digitally signed. |
| **Accreditation and permits** | Transmission is gated. EIS Certification is granted per server, sandbox testing precedes it, and a Permit to Transmit follows. BIR officials inidcated the accredited service provider model at the ECCP forum: taxpayers do not need to build direct BIR integration, and providers handle format conversion, signing and transmission. Accreditation guidelines were indicated as imminent at the time of our visit. |
| **Retrieval-ready archiving** | Invoice data must be stored in structured, machine-readable form and produced on demand. A PDF archive is not compliant. Reprinted electronic invoices must carry the word REPRINT on the face of the document, per BIR guidance at the forum. |

**The document hierarchy has inverted**

Under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act, the historical distinction between the Official Receipt and the Sales Invoice has been unified into a single invoice document. BIR officials went further at the ECCP forum: the electronic invoice replaces the paper invoice as the primary sales document, and official receipts are now secondary. Where a CAS permit is updated, a new Authority to Print should be applied for in respect of invoices rather than receipts.

**This matters more than it sounds. Process documentation written around the OR and SI split does not port forward, it gets rewritten. As one enterprise finance controller put it during our field work, they are not adding e-invoicing to an existing process, they are rewriting the process and adding e-invoicing at the same time.**

# **The CAS Gate**

**The upstream problem the market has not priced**

This section did not come from the survey. It came from the field, and it reframes every technical finding that follows. No enterprise can issue a compliant electronic invoice from a system that is not CAS-registered and acknowledged by the Bureau. That is not a ClearTax opinion. BIR's Information Systems Project Management Service indicated at the ECCP forum that CAS registration updates must be filed when a taxpayer upgrades its ERP.

**30 to 40%  of taxpayers at our Manila event said they were CAS-registered**

The remainder were still in process. Approximately 110 to 120 in-scope taxpayers attended.

**Why this is structurally worse than it looks**

* The gate re-closes on ERP migration. Migrating an ERP requires re-applying for CAS registration with the new system. This was confirmed three times independently during our field week, and indicated by BIR officials. Every enterprise mid-migration is therefore facing the gate twice.  
* It is per business unit and per system. Enterprises hold Acknowledgement Certificates across relevant business units, and organizations running a CAS alongside a POS system, or separate branch servers, carry the registration and certification burden on each.  
* It is invisible in a readiness self-assessment. A finance leader can reasonably rate their ERP as capable of generating structured data while the system that actually issues their invoices is a different, older, CAS-registered one. The survey cannot see this. The field work could.

**Three shapes of the same wound, observed in one week**

| Situation observed | Sector | The gap |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Accounts payable runs in a modern cloud ERP, but billing invoices must still be issued from a legacy system because the legacy system is the CAS-registered one. No visibility on when the new platform gets registered.** | Global information services, shared services arm | Cannot consolidate invoicing onto the target platform until registration catches up. The migration and the mandate are competing for the same window. |
| **An SAP instance that was never CAS-registered at all. Invoicing proceeds on loose-slip certification.** | Industrial packaging manufacturing | Starting the registration process from zero, inside the compliance window, with no prior filing history. |
| **Invoices generated from a cloud ERP that are, in the finance team's own description, internal documents only, because the generating system is not registered with the tax authority.** | Business process outsourcing, shared service unit | The documents being issued today do not qualify as invoices for tax purposes. This is a present-tense exposure, not a future one. |

*"Unfortunately, the invoice that we generate is not yet registered with the tax authority, so that PDF invoice becomes only an internal document. The first ask is really to help us set up those requirements."*

**Group Finance, business process outsourcing and shared services**

**What this means for sequencing**

**Every action item in this report assumes the CAS position is known. If it is not, that is the first workstream, ahead of vendor selection, ahead of the build, ahead of budget scoping. A finance function that buys an e-invoicing platform before resolving CAS registrationhas committed budget to a system it cannot yet legally use..**

There is a positive counter-example worth noting. One utility we met runs SAP S/4HANA on a clean-core architecture, is already CAS-registered, and holds the necessary Acknowledgement Certificates across its relevant business units. Their framing was that e-invoicing should not be treated as a standalone billing tool but as an integrated pipeline ensuring clean data flow and BIR recognition. That organization has removed the gate and can spend the remaining window on data quality and integration rather than on registration paperwork. It is the exception, and it is what good looks like.

# **The Timing Paradox**

**Why the market expects more time, and why that is still the expensive call**

Most practitioners we met in Manila expect the December 31, 2026 date to be reviewed. That expectation deserves to be reported rather than argued away, because a report that pretends the market holds no such view is a report finance leaders will not trust. Below is the market's reasoning, and then ours.

**The case for more time, as the market makes it**

* The regulation contemplates it. RR No. 26-2025, expressly reserves the power to further extend the compliance period as may be deemed necessary. The market reads that clause literally.  
* There is precedent. The original compliance date of March 14, 2026 was extended once, to December 31, 2026\. The stated reason was the operational adjustment required of taxpayers, including system reconfiguration.  
* Components are still to be published. The accreditation guidelines, the electronic sales reporting regulation, and the compliance date for the deferred segments were all unpublished at the time of our field work.  
* The sequencing is expressly conditional. Both RR No. 11-2025 and RR No. 26-2025 tie the reporting obligation to the establishment of the receiving system, which the market reads as a deliberately phased rollout.  
* The transition is large. Coverage spans a broad taxpayer population running very different system estates, from single-ERP enterprises to multi-server CAS and POS combinations.  
* Pilot participation is still in progress. Practitioners reported that a few pilot large taxpayers are yet to complete the sandbox testing.

**The case for acting anyway, which is stronger**

* Timing and obligation are different. The compliance date in force is December 31, 2026, and the Tax Code's penalty provisions attach to the obligation rather than to any system milestone. Practitioners reported substantial penalty assessments across the market during 2025\.  
* Audits look backwards. A penalty can be imposed at any time, and an audit conducted after the deadline reaches over the period that preceded it. A taxpayer who was non-compliant during a window that was later extended is not retroactively cured.  
* The programme is advancing on the record. Two Revenue Regulations were issued within an eighteen-month window, and the Bureau has published the supporting technical materials, including the EIS JSON file format and the EIS certification guidance with its sandbox and Permit to Transmit sequence. The direction of travel is documented, not inferred.  
* The gates are sequential and queued. CAS registration, EIS Certification per server, sandbox testing and the Permit to Transmit cannot be compressed on demand, and every taxpayer who waits joins the same queue.  
* The preparation is valuable independent of the date. Clean master data, mapped complex transactions, a resolved CAS position and a defined operating model are assets under any timeline.

*"The requisites have been in place for ten years and companies still have not fulfilled them. But prepare anyway. A penalty can be imposed at any time, and if the audit happens after the deadline, anyone can get penalised."*

**Partner, Big Four advisory firm, Manila**

*Our position. Our field research found a balanced practitioner view that documentation is expected to be in order by December 2026 with enforcement intensity expected to build through 2027\. We cannot corroborate that, and no published issuance supports a date beyond December 31, 2026\. What we can say is that the cost of preparing early and being right is a program delivered on schedule, while the cost of waiting and being wrong is a queue, an audit exposure, and a compressed timeline through gates that do not compress.* 

# **The B2C Blind Spot**

**Where Philippine volume actually sits, and the four-field problem nobody has scoped**

B2C is within the scope of the Philippine mandate. This was indicated independently by BIR officials at the ECCP forum and by advisory practitioners during our field week, and it is the single largest difference between the Philippine mandate and the business-to-business regimes that dominate European e-invoicing.

**Why this changes the volume calculation entirely**

European e-invoicing providers are paired with business-to-business flows. In Asia-Pacific and the Middle East, the volume sits in business-to-consumer. The enterprises we met in Manila bear this out. One facilities and food services group processes on the order of 1.2 million invoices. One home furnishings retailer is at roughly 4 million. Those counts are not generated by procurement cycles. They are generated at a point of sale, at a canteen counter, at a checkout, thousands of times a day.

| FROM THE FIELD   *A live scenario tested during our field work: a single site canteen serving 2,000 to 3,000 people in a day. Every one of those transactions is an invoice event under the mandate, and each must be generated, signed and queued for reporting. The B2B mental model does not survive contact with this volume.* |
| :---- |

**The four-discount problem**

Philippine law requires four distinct statutory discount categories to be captured and presented separately: senior citizen, person with disability, regular and special. Most ERP and point-of-sale systems in the market hold a single discount field.

**This is the most under-scoped technical requirement we encountered, and it has a hard limit that no vendor can engineer around. A middleware layer can add fields, transform them and map them into the required structure. It cannot invent data that the source system never captured. If the point-of-sale device records a single blended discount value, the statutory breakdown does not exist anywhere in the estate, and no amount of downstream processing will produce it.**

| The requirement | The common reality | What has to happen |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Four discount categories captured and presented as distinct fields: senior citizen, person with disability, regular, special.** | One discount field in the ERP or point-of-sale system, holding a blended value. | Source-system data capture must change. That means the POS vendor and the systems integrator, inside their own release cycles, before any e-invoicing integration can produce a compliant output. |

*Field observation on lead times. At one enterprise we met, regional IT approvals were described as slow and ERP field additions as long-running, with the discount field cited by the finance team as the specific example. That organization is simultaneously mid-migration between ERP generations and dependent on both a systems integrator and a separate point-of-sale vendor for any change. The four-field requirement therefore sits behind two vendor release cycles and one migration program. This is why it needs to be scoped in September rather than November.*

**The operational requirements B2C adds**

* Offline resilience. A point-of-sale device cannot assume connectivity. It must detect whether it is online, generate and report if so, and queue for transmission if not.  
* A reporting window that accommodates outages. BIR officials indicated at the forum that the three-day window functions as the natural buffer for connectivity interruptions, and that brief outages of minutes to hours will not trigger penalties (Unpublished guidance; not a statement of enforcement policy). Nonetheless, taxpayers are expected to maintain offline transaction logging, automatic retry, a manual exception process, and to notify the Bureau of systemic outages.  
* Bulk submission and retry logic at volume. At millions of invoices a year, exception handling cannot be manual, and staggered retry queues become a design requirement rather than a feature.  
* Audit-grade response trails. Time-stamped records of the Bureau's API responses are what protect a taxpayer in a later audit.

**The survey's Q5 result reads differently in this light. 86.4% of respondents have not mapped and tested complex transaction workflows. For a business-to-business enterprise that is a credit-note and intercompany problem. For the retail, food services, hospitality and consumer-facing enterprises that carry Philippine invoice volume, it is also a four-discount, offline-resilience and bulk-submission problem, and it sits in systems that finance does not directly control.**

# **The Central Thread**

***This survey does not reveal a market that misunderstands the mandate. It reveals a market that has not been funded to meet it, and a significant share that is not yet legally able to start.***

**What the dimension pattern reveals**

Regulatory Awareness and Phase Readiness scored highest at 36.4 and Technical Infrastructure 35.9. Those measure knowledge and systems. The dimensions measuring execution capacity scored lower: Process Maturity 23.2 and Organizational and Strategic Readiness 27.2. The 13.2-point spread maps onto the distance between knowing and doing.

This inverts the pattern ClearTax documented in the United Arab Emirates, where Organizational Readiness scored highest because it measured confidence. There is no comparable confidence inflation here. Philippine finance leaders rated their own budget, staffing and process positions honestly, and the ratings are low. Sentiment gaps close with education. Capacity gaps close only with budget and headcount decisions, which have longer lead times and belong to people outside the tax function.

# **The Coverage Trigger Analysis**

**Which limb of Section 3(A) pulls you into the first wave**

Most readiness surveys segment by revenue. In the Philippines that is the wrong axis, because coverage under RR No. 11-2025 is not primarily a function of size. It is a function of what a taxpayer does and what software it runs. The four limbs below each independently place a taxpayer in the December 31, 2026 wave.

| Limb | Trigger | What it captures | Readiness note |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **3(A)(1)** | E-commerce or internet transactions | Small, Medium and Large taxpayers selling over the internet, through own sites, platforms or any internet-based channel. Micro Taxpayers exempt. | Weakest cohort relative to priority. Named first in the regulation, scored 21.1. |
| **3(A)(2)** | Large Taxpayers Service jurisdiction | Any taxpayer under LTS jurisdiction, regardless of sector or activity. | Captures most banking, energy, telecom and large manufacturing respondents, including both lowest-scoring sectors. |
| **3(A)(3)** | EOPT Large Taxpayer classification | Taxpayers classified as Large under Republic Act No. 11976 and RR No. 8-2024. | A classification-based trigger operating independently of LTS registration. |
| **3(A)(4)** | Use of CAS, CBA or invoicing software | Any taxpayer using a Computerized Accounting System, Computerized Books of Accounts with electronic invoicing, or other invoicing software. | The broadest and least understood limb. Registration-agnostic, not size-based, and overrides deferral for exporters and RBEs. |

**Why 3(A)(4) is the one to check first**

* It is registration-agnostic. The phrase 'registered or accredited' in the Section 2(1) definition describes what a compliant output must become. It is not a gate limiting coverage to systems already registered. An unregistered CAS does not exempt a taxpayer, it adds an accreditation step, and field research confirms that step is where most of the market is stuck.  
* It is not size-based. The only size carve-out is the Micro Taxpayer exemption under Section 4\.  
* It overrides deferral. Exporters under Sections 106 and 108, and Registered Business Enterprises availing of incentives under Section 304(D), appear on the deferred list with an express exception for taxpayers falling under Section 3(A)(4). Running invoicing software pulls them into the first wave.

**If you issue invoices from software rather than from a printed book, start from the assumption that December 31, 2026 applies, and work to disprove it rather than the other way around. Then immediately establish whether the specific system that issues those invoices is CAS-registered, because that determines whether you can act on the answer.**

# **The Compliance Exposure Model**

**What Non-Compliance Actually Costs**

81.2% of respondents have no adequately scoped budget and 83.1% have no post go-live operations plan. Both reduce to the same failure: the cost of this mandate has not been quantified, so it has not been funded. This section sets out what belongs in that quantification.

**Statutory penalty exposure**

RR No. 11-2025 provides that violations are subject to the penalty provisions of the National Internal Revenue Code, as amended. Section 264 addresses failures relating to the issuance of invoices and receipts, and Section 264-A addresses failure to transmit sales data to the electronic sales reporting system.

*Specific peso amounts, computation basis and per-day multipliers under Sections 264 and 264-A are not reproduced here. Confirm the current figures against the statute and applicable Revenue Regulation before using them in a board paper, as penalty provisions have been amended across TRAIN, CREATE and CREATE MORE. Unverified, pending source confirmation.*

| FROM THE FIELD   *Field research materially raises the weight of this section. Practitioners reported billions of pesos in penalties imposed in the prior year, by an authority that was not itself operationally ready. The consistent professional advice we heard was that penalty exposure is real and largely independent of BIR's platform readiness, and that an audit conducted after the deadline reaches back across the period that preceded it.* |
| :---- |

**The six components of Philippine e-invoicing exposure**

| Component | How it accrues | Which gap drives it |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **CAS registration gap** | The upstream exposure. Invoices issued from an unregistered system may not qualify as valid invoices for tax purposes. This is a present-tense exposure for affected taxpayers, not a future one. | Field: 60 to 70% of taxpayers at our event were not yet CAS-registered. |
| **Failure to issue a compliant electronic invoice** | Attaches per document. A printed CAS output without electronic reporting capability is a traditional manual invoice and does not discharge the obligation. | Q3: 66.7% cannot generate compliant signed JSON; 28.8% cannot produce structured JSON at all. |
| **Failure to transmit sales data** | Attaches to the Section 237-A reporting obligation once the Bureau's system is established. Continuous rather than periodic. | Q6: 83.1% have no adequate plan for transmission monitoring. |
| **Unresolved rejections** | A rejected submission nobody processes is a transaction that was not reported. Exposure accumulates silently between rejection and discovery. | Q6: 44.6% have no response-handling or rejection-resolution plan at all. |
| **Certification and permit delay** | Certification is per server, sandbox testing precedes it, the Permit to Transmit follows. Practitioner guidance puts the post-accreditation certification journey at eight to ten weeks. | Q4: 73.4% have not decided build versus buy, which gates the entire sequence. |
| **Working capital and remediation** | Indirect but real. Invoice disputes and reconciliation backlogs delay collection, and manual remediation consumes unbudgeted finance and tax hours. | Q5: 86.4% have unmapped complex transactions, where validation failures concentrate. |

**The deduction on the other side of the ledger**

Republic Act No. 12066 and RR No. 11-2025 provide an additional deduction from taxable income for taxpayers mandated to issue electronic invoices and covered by electronic sales reporting, expressly including those who comply voluntarily. Only 4.7% of respondents have factored this in. Any exposure model that omits it overstates the net cost of compliance and understates the return on moving early.

*Confirm the quantum, computation basis and documentary requirements of the additional deduction against RR No. 11-2025 and any implementing issuance before relying on a specific figure. Unverified, pending source confirmation.*

# **The 128-Day Roadmap**

Working backwards from December 31, 2026\. The sequence is not aggressive, it is what the gates require. CAS registration precedes certification, certification precedes the Permit to Transmit, and none of them compresses on demand.

**The arithmetic that should worry you**

**The chain runs backwards from the Permit to Transmit, which requires completed certification, which requires passed sandbox testing, which requires a system that generates signed structured JSON, which requires a build, which requires a budget and a build-versus-buy decision, which requires knowing whether the issuing system is even CAS-registered. For the 48.4% with no budget approved and the 73.4% who have not chosen a path, the chain has not started. For the 60 to 70% not yet CAS-registered, it cannot start.**

Field research supplies two lead times that make the compression concrete. Practitioner guidance put the post-accreditation certification journey at roughly eight to ten weeks, and enterprise implementation guidance at eight to ten weeks plus a two-month buffer. 

Read sequentially, those lead times exceed the remaining window. They only fit if the workstreams run in parallel, which is the actual planning implication.

# **Five Strategic Priorities for the Run-In**

*The finance leaders who come through this transition well will not be distinguished by the software they buy. They will be distinguished by how early they knew where they stood.*

**01\.**

**Clear the registration gate before you buy anything**

CAS registration attaches to a system, not to a company. Identify the system that actually issues your invoices, not your flagship ERP. Then confirm whether it holds a current registration and Acknowledgement Certificate, and file what needs filing. Committing a budget to a platform before that is settled buys a system you cannot yet legally use.

**02\.**

**Sequence Leg 1 ahead of Leg 2 without discarding Leg 2**

December 31, 2026 requires one thing: A signed, structured invoice issued to the buyer. Reporting to the Bureau follows under a separate Revenue Regulation, once the receiving system is established. Build for the first obligation now, and architect so the second is a configuration change rather than a second programme.

**03\.**

**Fund it inside this planning cycle, not the next one**

The Philippine budget year is decided between July and October, and the CFO holds the budget. For a December 2026 obligation, the practical funding window is this quarter, and it closes with the planning cycle, not with the deadline. Scope CAS work, testing, training and post-go-live operations, and model the additional deduction.

**04\.**

**Treat B2C data capture as the binding technical constraint**

Philippine invoice volume sits in consumer-facing transactions, and the EIS JSON file format carries four discount fields, senior citizen, PWD, regular and special, each mandatory. A system holding one blended value cannot be mapped into compliance. Source-system change is therefore the critical path, and it sits behind POS vendor and integrator release cycles. Open that conversation this quarter.

**05\.**

**Prepare as though the date holds, because the obligation does not depend on the platform**

The market expects more time, and its reasons are on the record: One extension already granted, an express power to extend again under RR No. 26-2025, and guidance still pending publication. None of it changes the date in force. Audits examine prior periods, and the registration and certification gates queue. The timeline may be reviewed. The obligation is not. Prepare accordingly.
